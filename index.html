Policy Statement

GoHELP is committed to protecting sensitive customer data by enforcing strong authentication practices and comprehensive malware defences across all systems. This policy prohibits password reuse and mandates the use of approved, up-to-date antivirus/anti-malware software to reduce the risk of credential compromise and malicious code execution.

By implementing these controls, GoHELP aligns with current NIST guidance and regulatory obligations while fostering a culture of security awareness.

Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, consultants, and third parties who access GoHELP systems, applications, or data, including sensitive or regulated information.

It covers:

  • All user-chosen passwords for local and cloud accounts.
  • All GoHELP-owned or managed devices, including desktops, laptops, servers, and virtual machines (VMs).
  • Authorised BYOD devices that connect to corporate resources.

Definitions

Password reuse
Using the same password (or a minor variation) for multiple accounts or systems.
Compromised credential
A password that has appeared in known breach databases or is otherwise considered unsafe.
Password manager
An approved tool that generates, stores, and autofills unique passwords for each account.
Antivirus/anti-malware software
Approved security tools that scan for, detect, and remove malicious software.
Real-time protection
Continuous monitoring of files and processes to block threats as they occur.

Policy Details

This section sets out the core rules and expectations for password reuse and antivirus use and explains how staff must handle incidents and adopt secure behaviours in daily work.

It is designed to be clear, actionable, and aligned with current best practice so that employees know exactly what is required and why.

4.1 — Unique Password Requirement and Management

Users must:

  1. Create and maintain a unique password for every GoHELP account and service.
  2. Avoid reuse across work systems, personal accounts, or client environments.
  3. Use passwords of at least 15 characters when used as the sole authenticator.
  4. Use systems that accept passwords of 64 characters or more to allow passphrases.
  5. Use the company-provided password manager to generate and store unique passwords.

New passwords will be screened against breach databases to block commonly cracked or compromised values.

Manual memorisation or insecure storage, such as notes, spreadsheets, or emails, is not permitted.

4.2 — Malware Protection and Endpoint Security

All devices within scope must:

  • Have GoHELP-approved antivirus/anti-malware software installed, enabled, and configured.
  • Have real-time protection enabled before accessing corporate resources.
  • Automatically update security definitions and engine versions.
  • Undergo regular full system scans as scheduled by IT.
  • Have operating system and application patches applied promptly.

Users must not disable, tamper with, or bypass security controls without explicit written approval from IT/Security.

Responsibilities

All Users

  • Create and maintain unique passwords.
  • Use the approved password manager.
  • Enable MFA.
  • Keep antivirus protection enabled.
  • Run scans when prompted.
  • Report alerts or suspected compromise immediately.

IT/Security Team

  • Configure systems to enforce password length and breach screening.
  • Enforce MFA requirements.
  • Maintain password manager and antivirus services.
  • Monitor coverage, system health, and security detections.
  • Patch systems and investigate security incidents.

Managers

  • Ensure team compliance.
  • Support security training.
  • Facilitate device access for scans and updates.
  • Escalate repeated non-compliance to HR and Security.

Compliance

Compliance will be monitored through automated controls, including:

  • Password uniqueness checks.
  • Breach screening logs.
  • MFA enforcement.
  • Antivirus installation and health monitoring.
  • Definition age and scan results.
  • Tamper-event monitoring.

Non-compliance may result in mandatory retraining, temporary access restrictions, and, for repeated or deliberate violations, disciplinary action up to and including termination.

Exceptions

Exceptions may be granted only for legacy or specialised systems that cannot support current requirements.

Exceptions must be approved in writing by the CISO or delegate with a documented risk mitigation plan.

Compensating controls may include:

  • Network segmentation.
  • Additional monitoring.
  • Application allow-listing.

Exception requests must include:

  1. Justification for the exception.
  2. Expected duration.
  3. Compensating controls.

Exceptions will be reviewed at least quarterly.

Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed annually by the Information Security team, or sooner if there are:

  • Major regulatory changes.
  • Significant security incidents.
  • Updates to NIST guidance or industry standards.

Revisions will be communicated via email and posted on the GoHELP intranet, with version history maintained for audit purposes.
